Correcting a Malpresentation

Far From Help · Card A
NADIS · Wisconsin Visual Lambing Guide
RING THE VET FIRST — speakerphone in your chest pocket, video call if possible. Work together.

Prepare

Washed arm · long glove · lots of lubricant, re-lubed at every entry · cast her on her side (for a leg-back: missing-leg side uppermost; to repel a head: standing, or rear raised on a bale) · hand in a cone, enter slowly, work between contractions, pause when she strains.

Identify before pulling anything

Front leg: fetlock + knee bend the same way. Hind leg: they bend opposite ways. Tail near hooves = hind end. Trace every leg to its shoulder/hip — with twins, prove both legs are the same lamb’s.

Leg back (one front leg)

  1. Push the lamb gently back between strains.
  2. Follow shoulder → knee → cannon → fetlock.
  3. Cup the hoof in your palm (shields the uterus — the detail that matters most).
  4. Flex the knee; bring the cupped hoof up over the pelvic brim.
  5. Deliver with the traction rules below.

Head out, no legs (“hung lamb”)

  1. Keep the head moist; membranes off the nose.
  2. Repel the head back inside between strains — feels wrong, is right.
  3. Fetch each leg as above (cup every hoof).
  4. Snare behind the ears + through the mouth holds the head if it slips (never around the jaw alone).
  5. Can’t repel it in a few minutes → load & drive.

Hind legs first (soles up, no nose)

Don’t convert — deliver promptly: both legs, steady pull down toward her hocks with strains, brisk through the ribcage (cord pinches once hips engage). Clear the airway instantly; expect to resuscitate.

Head back · breech · twins tangled

Phone/video-guided only. Head back: repel, cup the muzzle (never eye sockets), bring it round, snare. Breech: repel rump, cup each hock’s hoof, flex up over the brim, deliver hind-first. Twins: repel the less-advanced lamb, deliver the engaged one. Two failed attempts = load and drive.

After delivery

Airway → rub hard → straw-tickle nostril. Cord intact 1–2 min. Check for another lamb. Iodine navel; colostrum early. Tell the vet what you did (antibiotic cover is their call).

TRACTION RULES: only during her strains · only two legs + head in place (or two hind legs) · downward arc toward her hocks — “down and around, never straight out” · 10–30 seconds of steady arms · never ropes to objects.
STOP = LOAD & DRIVE: two well-applied strains move nothing · you can’t identify what you feel · canal dry/swollen beyond lube · her pain escalating. A bedded trailer toward the vet is treatment, not defeat.

Stomach-Tubing a Lamb

Far From Help · Card B
OMAFRA · OSU · Cornell
USE WHEN: lamb ≥37 °C, can hold its head up and swallow, but won’t/can’t suck. NEVER TUBE: a lamb that can’t hold its head up or swallow · a lamb <37 °C older than ~5 h — that lamb gets the IP injection FIRST (Card C): “never stomach tube before an IP glucose injection — milk could kill the lamb” (SRUC).
  1. Measure and mark: nose → last rib.
  2. Sit; lamb upright on your lap, head natural and nose-forward (not stretched up).
  3. Wet the tip with colostrum. In at the side of the mouth, over the tongue, aiming slightly to the lamb’s left (food-pipe lies left of and behind the windpipe).
  4. Advance slowly. RIGHT = slides easily; the lamb may chew and swallow it; with a finger on the throat you feel two tubes (windpipe + yours). WRONG = resistance, coughing, struggling → withdraw completely, start again.
  5. Double-check: ear to the tube’s open end — airflow or crying = windpipe, out. Silence or stomach gurgle = correct.
  6. Syringe on, plunger out — warm colostrum by gravity (or very gentle push): 50 ml per kg over ~5 minutes (≈200 ml for a 4 kg lamb). Never fast.
  7. Crimp the tube firmly, then withdraw in one smooth motion (no drips into the airway).
  8. Warm dry pen · recheck 30–60 min · repeat feeds toward ~200 ml/kg by 24 h if still not sucking.
ABORT IF: any cough or gag at any point (withdraw, restart) · you cannot pass it to the mark after two calm attempts (ring the vet — video call, they can see your angle).

IP Glucose Injection — Cold-Lamb Rescue

Far From Help · Card C
FAS Scotland · SRUC · Eales & Small 1982
USE ONLY WHEN ALL THREE ARE TRUE: lamb below 37 °C · older than ~5 hours · cannot swallow / hold its head up. A lamb that can swallow gets warmed + tubed instead (Card B). Do not warm this lamb first — warming without energy kills it.

Prepare

Warmed 20% glucose/dextrose, 10 ml per kg — a 4–5 kg lamb takes the full ~45–50 ml. From the kit, or mix: 20 g glucose powder in hot pre-boiled water made up to 50 ml (40%), then dilute half-and-half with warm pre-boiled water → ≈20%, warm-on-the-wrist (never cold). 50–60 ml syringe · new sterile 1″ (25 mm) needle.

Inject

  1. Ring the vet (speaker, pocket). Draw up the warmed solution.
  2. Hang the lamb by both front legs (helper ideal; alone: forelegs in one hand, lamb’s back against your legs). Gravity drops the organs clear.
  3. Site: ~2.5 cm (1″) below the navel, ~1 cm (½″) to the side.
  4. Full needle in at 45°, angled toward the tail head — two faint pops (skin, wall).
  5. Inject the full dose slowly and steadily. Withdraw, rub, iodine the site.

Then — in this order

  1. NOW warm: warm-air box ~35–40 °C; check every 20–30 min; recheck lamb temp at 30 min. Expect it standing in 30–60 min.
  2. Once ≥37 °C and swallowing: tube 50 ml/kg colostrum (Card B).
  3. Re-mother in a pen; confirm sucking.
DO NOT: inject a lamb that can swallow (tube it) · use cold solution · reuse needles between lambs · inject at or forward of the navel itself. Not clearly improving 30–60 min after warming → ring again / drive.

Replacing a Bearing + Retainer

Far From Help · Card D
Farmers Weekly · Northland Vets NZ · Ag Victoria
RING THE VET FIRST — they may say “bring her in”, or talk you through this while you work.
CHECK FIRST — DO NOT REPLACE IF: the tissue is torn, black or cold (a vet must judge if it’s salvageable — ring/drive) · she is actually lambing behind it (water bag, feet or tail palpable through the cervix) — that is a lambing emergency now, ring immediately.
  1. Two people if you can: one holds her standing against a rail. Raise her hindquarters (bale under the back legs, or rear held up wheelbarrow-style) — it makes everything easier.
  2. Clean: plenty of clean warm water with mild antiseptic — dilute iodine to a “weak-tea colour” (Northland Vets). All dirt and bedding off. No scrubbing.
  3. Lubricate the whole surface generously.
  4. Replace: first lift the mass up toward her tail — this un-kinks the bladder; she will often pass urine and the ball shrinks. Then, with the flat of a clean hand or a closed fist — never fingertips — slow, steady, patient pressure. Hold your ground when she strains; gain between strains. It goes back gradually, then all at once — follow it in so the tip is fully un-inverted.
  5. Retain: bearing spoon/T-piece — tongue inside the vagina, wings tied firmly to the wool of her flanks with the tapes — or a prolapse harness fitted per its instructions.
  6. Aftercare: ring the clinic and report — pain relief and usually antibiotic cover are warranted (vet-supplied in NZ; ask about pre-supplied cover for your distance before lambing). Check the retainer twice daily for slip and rubbing.
  7. At lambing: she can lamb past a spoon (it pushes out) but watch her closely; a harness must be released. Mark her — bearings recur. Do not breed her again.
WHILE WAITING / DRIVING: pen her quietly · keep the tissue clean and moist — clean towel soaked in clean lukewarm water held over it · keep other stock and flies off · never let her run or be chased.
